Related Works

Data-driven approaches to constitutive modeling in solids mechanics have increasingly leveraged advances in machine learning, spanning generative modeling, symbolic equation discovery, recurrent architectures, and attention-based neural operators.

## Generative Models for Microstructure Synthesis

One line of research applies deep generative models to the synthesis of material microstructures. Synthesizing controlled porous media microstructures has been approached using generative adversarial networks combined with reinforcement learning, with graph-theoretic descriptors—such as graph transitivity, density, average clustering coefficient, and efficiency—used to characterize the topology of the generated pore networks [0]. These graph metrics quantify structural properties such as connectivity and local clustering, providing a way to describe and compare the pore network topology of generated microstructures [0].

## Symbolic and Equation Discovery Methods

A complementary direction focuses on discovering interpretable constitutive laws directly from data through graph-based symbolic regression. This approach represents candidate constitutive equations as graphs assembled from predefined templates and operator sets (e.g., addition, multiplication, power), constraining graph depth and structure to keep the search space tractable while still allowing flexible equation forms [4][2]. Candidate graphs are evaluated against experimental datasets from multiple materials, with material-specific parameters fitted via optimization (e.g., L-BFGS) and the mean error across datasets used as a generalizability-promoting fitness metric [2]. An evolutionary search strategy—incorporating crossover, mutation, selection of top-performing graphs, and periodic "extinction" events to maintain diversity—is used to avoid premature convergence to local optima [2][4]. This graph-based equation discovery framework aims to move beyond purely empirical or black-box models toward new, interpretable constitutive laws [2].

## Broader Landscape of Data-Driven Constitutive Modeling

A review of data-driven constitutive laws situates these methods within a broader taxonomy of machine learning tools applied to solid mechanics, including feature extraction and dimensionality reduction techniques (e.g., PCA, autoencoders, restricted Boltzmann machines), generative frameworks (variational autoencoders, GANs, normalizing flows such as RealNVP, and diffusion models), and inverse-problem approaches using conditional invertible neural networks [1]. This review also highlights open methodological challenges, including the design of test methods and specimen geometries to ensure parameter identifiability, the role of uncertainty quantification in determining data requirements and assessing model reliability, and the need for curated benchmark datasets—proposing, for example, open datasets based on RVE-generated homogenization data for composite microstructures and full-field displacement data from experiments [3]. These considerations frame the evaluation criteria against which newer data-driven approaches, including generative and symbolic methods, should be benchmarked [3].

## Sequential and History-Dependent Modeling

For path- and history-dependent materials, recurrent neural network architectures have been employed to capture time-dependent constitutive behavior. Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) have been used in cooperative data-driven modeling frameworks, motivated by their simplicity and effectiveness relative to Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, while both architectures address the vanishing/exploding gradient problems that limit standard RNNs in learning long-range temporal dependencies relevant to mechanics problems [6]. Building on this history-dependent modeling paradigm, more recent work has introduced attention-based neural operators for path-dependent constitutive modeling. The History-Aware Neural Operator employs a self-attention mechanism, computing query, key, and value representations for each input in a sequence and aggregating value vectors via softmax-weighted attention scores to produce context-dependent representations that emphasize the most predictive parts of the loading history [5][7]. This attention formulation, expressed in matrix form as a scaled dot-product operation, allows the model to dynamically weight different points in a material's deformation history, extending prior work that incorporated attention primarily for spatial modeling within Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) architectures to the temporal/history dimension relevant for path-dependent materials [7].

## Synthesis

Collectively, these works illustrate a diversification of data-driven strategies for constitutive modeling and microstructure design: generative adversarial and reinforcement learning methods for controllable microstructure synthesis [0], evolutionary graph-based symbolic regression for discovering interpretable governing equations [2][4], recurrent and attention-based neural architectures for capturing history-dependent material response [6][5][7], and overarching reviews that catalog available tools while identifying open challenges in identifiability, uncertainty quantification, and benchmark dataset curation [1][3]. Together, they suggest a field moving from purely black-box predictive models toward architectures and discovery frameworks that balance predictive accuracy, interpretability, and robustness to path dependency and microstructural variability.
